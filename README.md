# Proposal for Enhanced Classroom Privacy and Intellectual Property Protections at Virginia Tech

This repository contains a strategic policy proposal aimed at updating university guidelines to protect faculty intellectual property and student privacy in digital spaces, while maintaining a commitment to open academic inquiry and free expression.

---

## 1. Rationale & Legal Framework

All lectures, slides, syllabi, and course materials represent the scholarly and creative dedication of our faculty. The Copyright Act of 1976 provides the foundational framework for current copyright law (*U.S. Copyright Office, 2025, para. 1*). Under this law, protection extends to "original works of authorship fixed in any tangible medium of expression" (*17 U.S.C. § 102a, 2024*), granting copyright owners "the exclusive rights to do and to authorize" reproduction and distribution (*17 U.S.C. § 106, 2024*). 

Filming active classroom lectures and uploading them to social media platforms without consent directly infringes upon these rights. More importantly, it compromises our learning environment:
* **Hinders Student Participation:** When students face the risk of appearing in public videos, they become hesitant to participate in discussions or ask questions.
* **Restricts Open Academic Inquiry:** Faculty members become less willing to share their developing research or unrefined ideas.
* **Violates Personal Privacy:** Unauthorized filming captures students without their knowledge or explicit consent.

> *"When classrooms lack privacy, we lose the freedom to learn. Let's protect our campus and preserve the open inquiry central to the academic integrity of our university."*

---

## 2. Proposed Policy Accommodations

The following measures are designed to be precise and balanced, while remaining strictly neutral. They build upon established provisions of **Policy 5000**, which stipulates that the *"use of specific site locations, such as the Drillfield, dining halls, residence halls, academic buildings, Library Plaza, War Memorial Chapel, Moss Arts Center, Recreational Sports facilities, and Athletic Department facilities will be in accordance with guidelines for those areas"* (*Shepard, 2024, p. 1*).

* **Define Digital Recordings:** This policy ensures that Policy 5000 is clearly updated to define content creation and digital recordings. This will make it easier to distinguish personal photos taken from commercial, ongoing, or disruptive vlogging.
* **Clear Distinctions of Facilities:** Locations such as residence hall corridors, libraries, dining halls, and classrooms are designated as indoor spaces where recording recognizable people without permission and unauthorized commercial production are not permitted.
* **Protection for Employees:** Restrict recordings of facility, dining, and other university employees while performing their duties without prior consent.
* **Mandatory Signage:** Implement clear notices at entrances of libraries, gyms, dining halls, and other campus facilities that state that recording staff members or patrons without prior consent is prohibited.
* **Syllabus Standardization:** Add a privacy rule to every single syllabus where recording classroom lectures and uploading them to digital platforms without consent is prohibited. It gives instructors clear authority to address violations and protect any approved accommodations for disability.
* **Permits for Commercial Filming:** A permit should be required for sponsored or monetized productions within university facilities.
* **Outdoor Enforcement:** Authorize facility personnel to instruct non-affiliated individuals engaging in unauthorized recording in restricted areas to vacate the premises, escalating to the Virginia Tech Police Department upon non-compliance.

---

## 3. Conclusion & Call to Action

Virginia Tech's dedication to free expression and its responsibility for safety, privacy, and academic integrity are not in conflict; both rely on clear, well-defined rules. If we wait for a serious incident to occur before we act, the university will merely be reacting to a crisis rather than proactively protecting its students. The proposed updates are neutral, targeted, and firmly grounded in established policy. 

We respectfully call upon our campus leaders—the **Virginia Tech Commission on Student Affairs**, the **Faculty Senate**, and the **Student Government Association (SGA)**—to consider and pass resolutions forwarding these recommended policy changes to the Executive Vice President and Chief Operating Officer.
